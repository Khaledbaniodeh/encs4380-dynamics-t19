The field guide

Understand it. Then explore it.

A change.A response.

The world changes. A sensor takes time to follow. Dynamics Lab makes that response visible through four browser-based experiments. Start here — no sensor hardware or prior experience needed.

The idea, in one example

A warmer room.
A reading that catches up.

Move a thermometer from a cool room to a warm one. The temperature changes, but its reading rises gradually. Understanding that response helps you judge how closely a reading follows a changing measurement.

Input
The quantity being measured — here, the room’s actual temperature.
Output
The sensor’s response — here, the temperature it reports.

  1. Choose a change

    A sudden step, a steady ramp, or a repeating wave. Each asks the sensor to follow in a different way.

  2. Watch the response

    Adjust one setting at a time. See how quickly the output follows, settles, or overshoots.

  3. Predict. Then check.

    Make a prediction before changing a setting. Compare it with the result to test your understanding.

Two ways to read the gap.

Reading error · output units

How far away?

The difference between the ideal reading and the actual output at the same instant. For a calibrated thermometer, that might be a reading 2 °C below the room temperature.

Ramp time lag · seconds

How far behind?

During steady ramp tracking, compare when the ideal reading and the output reach the same value. This time gap describes the response after its initial transient; the sensor starts responding immediately.
