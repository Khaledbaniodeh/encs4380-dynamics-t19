Behind the experiment

One plant. One delayed measurement.

Physical states

Plant: τ · dy/dt + y = K · u
Sensor: τs · dym/dt + ym = y
Controller error: e = r − ym

Controller & numerical solution

u_raw = Kp · e + Ki · ∫e dt − Kd · dym/dt
u = clip(u_raw, minimum, maximum)
All initial states are zero.
Python advances the states using our own RK4 loop.

D acts on the measurement, avoiding a derivative kick at the setpoint step. After that step the setpoint is constant. τs = 0 uses ideal feedback. Like P1, D acts on the measured output and the control command has limits with anti-windup. P2 computes the measurement derivative directly from its noise-free sensor model, rather than using P1’s filtered finite difference.
